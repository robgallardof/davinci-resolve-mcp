# Creator polish: camera, attention and cuts

Use when the brief asks for professional polish, more dynamic camera changes, better focus, retention,
or a MrBeast-inspired edit. These are adaptable editorial choices, not mandatory effects.
Default to restrained polish; an explicit energetic brief permits stronger accents. Calm interviews,
demonstrations and emotional scenes still need breathing room. Do not impose this style on every job.

## From reference to decisions

For a supplied reference, inspect the actual available video and record observed timecodes, shot sizes,
reason for each cut, caption behavior and sound. Distinguish observation from interpretation. If only
an interview summary is accessible, do not claim to have measured its edits or watched the episode.

The Colin & Samir interview's published notes identify storytelling (27:50), imitation/Beastification
(38:15) and short-form differences (48:22). These support researching creative intent, not a universal
cut frequency or a proprietary MrBeast preset. The practical recipe below is our editorial synthesis.

1. Make the opening promise concrete with available footage. Establish the subject and stakes early.
2. Sequence setup → progress/obstacle → consequence → payoff. Remove repetition and logistical waiting;
   keep context needed to understand why a result matters. Never manufacture reactions or stakes.
3. Alternate action, meaningful detail and genuine reactions. Re-establish geography after tight shots.
4. Increase contrast at the payoff, then allow recovery. Sustained intensity erases the emphasis.
5. If the user supplies retention analytics, map dips/spikes to actual moments before revising. A spike
   may indicate confusion, not success. Do not promise higher retention without audience evidence.

## Camera language and focus

Choose wide/context → medium/action → close/reaction deliberately. Prefer a real alternate angle when
available; a digital crop is a reframing of the same camera, not a newly captured angle. For multicam,
verify sync using shared action/sound and maintain one clear audio master before switching angles.

For a slightly more dramatic emphasis, audition an extra crop of roughly 1.08–1.15 over the established
framing, with an occasional 1.15–1.20 accent if resolution and composition allow. These are starting
points, not new tool parameters or mandatory ranges. Use `preview_motion` and the actual style schema;
check effective crop including portrait conversion, stabilization, rotation and existing manual zoom.
Source dimensions alone do not guarantee usable detail after compression or missed focus.

Land the crop on the important face, hand, product or action. Preserve eyeline, lead room and relevant
hands, rather than centering every face mechanically. For moving subjects, inspect the path between
start/middle/end samples; avoid chasing detector jitter. Use a wider stable crop when tracking is uncertain.
Review both sides of each jump for pose, gaze, scale and direction. Cut at a meaningful phrase/action;
use easing for a push, a deliberate instantaneous change for a punch. Do not mix them accidentally.

Do not equate framing with optical focus. Prefer the sharp take; sharpening cannot recover missing
detail. Background blur/rack focus requires suitable footage, masks/tracking and a supported execution
path; inspect hair/hand edges and do not imply a crop or LUT performs refocusing. Do not apply artificial
blur, stabilization or speed ramps by default. Verify their capabilities before planning execution.

## A practical intensity brief

| Intent | Camera | Text | Sound/color |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restrained professional | Stable compositions, occasional motivated close-up | Clean phrases, subtle entry | Clear dialogue, natural match |
| Dynamic creator | Contrast wide/close at ideas and reactions | Active word or brief pop on key ideas | Selective accents, restrained look |
| High-energy on request | Short bursts at verified action/payoffs, recovery between them | Impact cue at the peak | Motivated SFX, no continuous escalation |

Record the chosen intent, moments, subject/crop, transition and caption treatment in the edit plan.
Use supported styles rather than inventing a `MrBeast` style or assuming these choices are implemented
as automatic presets. Follow `transitions-and-flow.md`, caption design and camera-aware finishing.
After changing cuts, invalidate downstream word timings and audio cues. Watch/listen to a short
representative section before applying the vocabulary throughout the piece.
